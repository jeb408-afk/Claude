## Read-through (for teleprompter)

This is Kristaps Porzingis. Seven foot three. One of the most skilled big men in basketball.
In 2025, he was diagnosed with POTS. Postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome.
When he stands up, his heart rate can hit nearly 130 beats per minute.
POTS means a jump of 30 or more, just from standing.
If it can slow down a seven-footer in the NBA, think about everyone else who has it.
POTS is one of the most reported problems in long COVID.
Right now, about 17 million American adults have it.
79 percent say it limits their daily activities.
Between 2 and 4 million are out of work because of it.
That's about 170 billion dollars in lost wages. Every year.
Harvard economist David Cutler put the total cost at 3.7 trillion dollars.
For an illness most people think is over.
Porzingis has NBA doctors. Most people with long COVID don't.
Follow for more.

## Accuracy notes
- Do not say or imply Porzingis's POTS came from COVID. No public source says that. The script only says POTS is common in long COVID.
- "30 or more" is the standard adult POTS threshold (HR rise of 30+ bpm within 10 min of standing).
- 17M is a 2024 figure; say "about" and keep the date in the on-screen source.
- $170B is the low end of Brookings' range ($170B to $230B).

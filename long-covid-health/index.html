    Resting heart rate and HRV

    The two most sensitive early-warning signals for overexertion. A rising RHR or falling HRV for 2+ days is a cue to rest. Resting HR is calculated from your raw heart rate: the 25th percentile of awake minutes with no steps in the surrounding minutes. Overnight is your average while asleep. Apple's own value is used only on days without enough readings. HRV comes from Apple, since it needs beat-to-beat data.

    Heart rate spikes and orthostatic signs

    A spike is a rise of 30+ bpm above your average from 6 to 15 minutes earlier. A spike with under 30 steps around it happened while you were still, which is the pattern seen when standing up with POTS. Needs readings every few minutes or more often.

    With "Leave out sleep" on, heart rate while asleep is removed from daily HR, threshold minutes, tachycardia, spikes and resting HR. Sleep comes from Health sleep records (asleep or in bed); on nights without them it is estimated from the longest quiet stretch with low heart rate.

    Not medical advice. These are pattern-finding tools based on published pacing approaches (Workwell Foundation heart-rate pacing, Bateman Horne Center guidance). Thresholds are estimates; your clinician can set personal limits, especially if you take beta blockers or ivabradine, which lower heart rate.

    Steps from iPhone and Apple Watch are de-duplicated by taking the higher source each hour. Heart rate is reduced to one value per minute so workouts do not outweigh background readings.
